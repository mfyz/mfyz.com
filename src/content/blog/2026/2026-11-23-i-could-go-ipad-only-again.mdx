I've been doing more of my day-to-day work with agents on my iPad. I can go days at a time, sometimes a whole week, without reaching for my laptop. I can see that becoming my everyday setup soon.

The time it felt most natural was also the most fun to tell, and it started with a tire.

I was camping upstate when I stopped at a restaurant to grab something to eat. The week before, my car had failed its inspection because one tire was worn down enough to need replacing. While I was eating, I wondered if tires might be cheaper outside the city and if there was a shop near the campsite. I needed to find a tire that fit my car, could handle its weight, and was available to install. I had my iPad with me, so I started looking over cellular.

## Tire shopping in split screen

I asked Codex to find tire shops nearby. One shop had 34 options that fit my car, so I asked it to put them in a table with prices and the dates I could book an installation for each tire model. Then I wanted to know what I'd actually pay after discounts and installation fees. Then I wanted to hear from people who had put those same tires on the same car. Codex looked through Reddit threads and added what owners had to say about things like road noise and tread life.

I had ChatGPT open on the left and the document on the right. I could sort by price or filter by installation day, then ask another question without losing my place in the table. As Codex found more, it updated the same document.

The Codex session was running on my Mac. My projects and Obsidian vault were already there, including the car's make, model, and year, and the tire photos I'd taken earlier that week. I could start with “help me find a tire” and go straight into comparing options.

## I went all in on the iPad once before

When the first iPad Pro came out, I [used it as my only computer for seven months](/ipad-pro-raspberry-pi-portable-development-environment-on-mobile/), and I loved it. I could do email, manage projects, and write from almost anywhere. Then I'd need to run Node, open a Sketch file, or move files between tools, and I'd be back to trying to make my desktop workflow fit. I set up remote machines and later experimented with a Raspberry Pi. I eventually went back to a regular computer.

I've been [writing about working from wherever I am](/digital-nomads/) for years. I used to put a lot of thought into [how I'd get online while traveling](/having-reliable-internet-while-travelling-every-corner-of-the-world/). More recently I got a [foldable phone working as a dev setup](/folding-phones-on-the-go-dev-setup/) with SSH and a Linux VM. I'd even wondered in that post how long I'd last if I tried the iPad experiment again.

## I could see myself packing just the iPad

I do more of my work through agents now. I'm reading, asking questions, checking their work, and deciding what to do next. The Mac still runs the tools. I can leave it at home and bring a screen big enough to read the work while I talk to the agent. With cellular, that was enough to spend an afternoon working the way I do at my desk.

I'd still like more freedom to run things locally on the iPad. What feels local to me has changed. My files and tools live on the Mac, and I can work with them from the screen I have with me. If I were packing for a few weeks away, I think I'd be comfortable leaving the laptop at home.
