Orchestrating agents cranks my brain up to 10.

I can have one agent building something while another investigates a problem. Meanwhile, I'm reviewing a plan and thinking about what to send out next. It's fun. I can get so much more done this way.

But my head is keeping track of all of it. What each agent is doing, why I asked for it, which decisions are waiting on me. The agents carry their own context, and I carry the context across the whole thing.

When I used to build one thing at a time, implementation gave my thinking a pace. I'd spend a while working through one problem. Now I can move between several problems as fast as the agents bring results back. There is always something ready for my attention.

After weeks of running like that, I start feeling slower. It gets harder to hold everything in my head. Even work I enjoy starts feeling tiring.

A walk or an evening away helps with the day's fatigue. What I'm talking about builds up over a longer stretch. I need a few days away from orchestrating things so my brain can stop keeping all those threads warm.

Going fully offline helps. No screens, no access to the agents, including through [my latest wearable obsession, Pebble Index 01](/pebble-index-1-always-available-agents/). Having an agent within reach is useful, but it makes stepping away harder. Even my phone is enough of a temptation. One quick interaction can get my head back into the whole thing.

My version is taking a break from both input and output: nothing to review, no instructions to send, no need to turn a thought into a task. Give my brain a little time to think on its own.

What's started working for me is spending several days in nature, camping and doing physical work on a piece of land I've been working on. Other weekends, it's a few days of kids' activities, like attending their chess tournaments. I'm occupied with something I care about, away from the agent work long enough to slow down. When I'm back, I'm back in the agent grind pretty quickly :)

I still love working with agents. I'm learning that I need these mini sabbaticals too, even when the work is going well. Especially then, because that's when it's easiest to keep going.
